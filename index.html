01 Brand & Logo Design

Identity that holds up at any size

Good branding doesn't start with a font choice. It starts with understanding who the business is for, what it needs to say without saying it, and how it should feel the moment someone sees it — on a shop sign, a business card or a phone screen.

I work through concepts before I touch colour: sketching shapes, testing how a mark reads small, checking it still works in black and white before it ever gets its final palette.

Every logo I deliver is built clean and vector-first, so it scales from a favicon to a billboard without losing a single edge. No blurry exports, no guesswork for whoever prints it next.

The goal is always the same: a mark that's simple enough to remember, distinct enough to stand out, and solid enough to still make sense five years from now.

02 Social Media Design

Graphics built to stop the scroll

A feed post has about a second to earn attention before someone swipes past. That means every layout has to be readable instantly — clear hierarchy, confident type, colour that does the work before the caption does.

I design for how people actually consume content: posts, carousels, reels covers and story templates that stay consistent with a brand's identity instead of feeling like one-off graphics.

Beyond the visuals, I pay attention to what performs — sizing things correctly for each platform, keeping key text inside safe zones, and building templates that are easy to reuse for the next post.

The result is content that looks intentional, not improvised — built to grow an account, not just decorate it.

03 Apparel & Print

From raster to print-ready vector

A lot of apparel design work isn't about creating from a blank page — it's about taking a rough photo, an old logo scan, or a low-resolution file and rebuilding it as clean, scalable vector art that a printer can actually use.

I trace and rebuild artwork by hand rather than relying on automated tracing, because a shirt print has to hold sharp edges and flat, accurate colour separations — not jagged lines or muddy gradients that fall apart on fabric.

I work with the practical side of printing too: colour counts, placement, and file formats that match what the print method actually needs, whether that's screen printing, DTF or a poster run.

The goal is simple — artwork that looks exactly as sharp on the finished product as it did on screen.

04 About Haroon

Self-taught, detail-first

I'm Haroon Mughal, a graphic designer working under the name @haroonmughalgfx. I'm largely self-directed — most of what I know about design, vector work and client delivery, I built by practicing on real work rather than waiting for a classroom to hand it to me.

Alongside design, I'm currently studying at Swedish College, which keeps me sharp on the technical and analytical side of things while I grow the design business on my own time.

What I care about isn't chasing every trend — it's understanding what a brand or a client actually needs, then building something clean, usable and durable around that. A logo or a print file only matters if it still works once it leaves my screen.

Outside of client work, I'm usually practicing: new styles, new software, new ways to make a design process faster without cutting corners on quality.

That's more or less the mix: self-taught, still learning, and genuinely interested in getting better with every project.
